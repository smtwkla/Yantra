## Views on the open questions (for gate B)

- **Q1, when *overdue* fires — (a) is sound for the floor.** Three reasons. (1) The plant's own rule (Maintenance-Philosophy § 7, decided R03 Q1.1) says a job is late only after the window; an alert called *overdue* before that would contradict the PM list, which still says *Due* with the days past. (2) From the due day the job is already on the assignee's phone (*due* alert), in *Me › Open work* and on the PM list's *Due* tab with "N days past"; the engineer looks at that list daily (TPM: the planner's morning look), so the alert is for the exception, not the routine. (3) Pushing every job to the engineers at the due point is the daily noise D8 rejects. The one real gap is a job with no reachable assignee (none, or a contractor) on a long-interval plan: nobody is pushed for up to 37 days. That is bounded by the list and by R03.14's overdue list, and the daily summary email (Plan § 4.7, phase 5) is the right place to list jobs due; not a reason to move the alert. Keep (a).
- **Q2, a started job — (a).** A job half done for days is a supervision matter (R03.14), and alerting the person who is doing it helps nobody.
- **Q3, the manager in *overdue* — (a).** By key is S074's rule and R03.9 gives the manager the actions; a manager with *all units* at one site gets one push per overdue job, which is what he should see.

## Findings

### BR1-01 — minor — § 5.2: a change of assignee after *due* was sent has no rule

**Problem.** The re-arm table lists five changes but not the one `pm_jobs._refresh` also makes: `assignee_id` (an `assignee_changed` line, when the engineer changes the plan's assignee while the job is open). The markers compare only the due point, so the new assignee gets no *due* alert; he hears first at *overdue*, if ever (a monthly job: 4 days after the due date). The coder has to decide alone whether to send *due* again, and the user would meet the choice in the code.

**Why it matters.** This is the case S074 answered with *Countermeasure for you* on a change of owner (D5 there). Here it is a judgement call: the job is in the new assignee's *Me › Open work* at once, so a push is a convenience, not the only channel.

**Options.**
- (a) **No alert on an assignee change; say so in § 5.2's table and in § 1's out-of-scope list** — recommended. One line: "The assignee changes | nothing: the job is in the new assignee's *Me › Open work*; an *assigned to you* PM kind is a later step." Keeps the markers keyed on the due point only, and S102 adds no route code.
- (b) Re-arm *due* on an assignee change: the markers would also have to hold the assignee alerted (a fifth column), or `_refresh` would clear the due marker, which is the "a forgotten path loses alerts" design D3 rejected.
- (c) A third kind *PM job assigned to you*, queued by `_refresh` and the plan save: the S074 D5 shape, but it puts push work on the plan-save path D4 keeps free, and is a new kind the roadmap did not ask for.
